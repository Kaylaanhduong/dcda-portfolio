My Hometown Map: Saigon

Kayla Duong | DCDA 40833 Skills Portfolio | Spring 2026

🗺️ Hometown Map - Saigon Locations

An interactive map showcasing 12 meaningful locations in Saigon, Vietnam. Each marker represents a place that has shaped who I am— from schools and favorite restaurants to cultural landmarks and recreational spots where I've spent countless hours with friends and family. Click on any marker to discover more about that location, including photos and personal stories about why each place matters to me.

💡 Tip: Use the navigation controls on the map to zoom in/out and explore different areas of Saigon. The legend in the bottom-left corner shows you what each colored icon represents!

About This Map

Saigon is more than just my hometown—it's a living, breathing city full of energy, contradictions, and endless possibilities. This map is my way of sharing a piece of my identity through the places that have shaped me. Each of the 12 locations represents a chapter in my story, organized into five categories: Schools & Educational Institutions, Landmarks, Restaurants & Cafes, Culture & History, and Recreation.

I created this map not just as a portfolio project, but as a love letter to my city. It's my way of saying thank you to Saigon for the education, the experiences, the friendships, and the countless moments—both big and small—that have shaped my life.

Reflection & Analysis

Design Inspiration

The design of this map was inspired by my desire to create an interactive storytelling experience. Rather than just showing a list of coordinates, I wanted to bring each location to life with images, descriptions, and visual hierarchy. The five location categories emerged naturally from the types of places that are most meaningful to me, and I chose emoji icons because they're universally recognizable and add a playful, personal touch to the cartography.

I intentionally chose a custom Mapbox basemap style that feels modern and clean, allowing the markers to stand out. The color coding system—blue for schools, orange for landmarks, green for restaurants, purple for culture, and red for recreation— helps users quickly understand what they're looking at without needing to read the legend every time. The legend itself was placed in the bottom-left corner following standard cartographic conventions.

Cartographic Challenges

One of my biggest cartographic challenges was determining the exact coordinates for each location. Vietnamese addresses are formatted differently than addresses in English-speaking countries, which made automated geocoding unreliable. When I initially used the Mapbox Geocoding API, several locations were placed in completely wrong areas—some even ended up outside of Saigon!

To solve this, I manually verified each location using Google Maps and collected the precise latitude/longitude coordinates. This manual verification process took time, but it ensured accuracy. Another challenge was deciding on the zoom level and center of the map—I wanted to show all 12 locations while keeping the view zoomed in enough to see Saigon clearly, so I settled on a zoom level of 12 and centered it on the heart of District 1.

I also grappled with the popup design. I wanted to include images for each location to make it visually rich, but I had to balance image file sizes with loading performance. Ultimately, I set a fixed height for popup images and used responsive sizing to keep popups from becoming overwhelming on smaller screens.

Technical Challenges

The biggest technical challenge was switching from Folium (Python-based mapping library) to Mapbox GL JS. My initial approach used Folium with a Mapbox tileset, but the custom Mapbox style wasn't displaying correctly through Folium's TileLayer implementation. After troubleshooting and researching, I realized that Mapbox GL JS was the proper solution for using modern Mapbox custom styles.

This required me to rewrite the entire map implementation from scratch using JavaScript instead of Python. I had to hardcode all 12 location objects with their coordinates, descriptions, and image URLs directly into the HTML/JavaScript. While less elegant than a data-driven Python script, this approach gave me more direct control over the map rendering and styling.

Another technical challenge was managing popups with embedded images. I had to ensure that images had proper error handling (using the onerror attribute) in case any image URLs broke or became inaccessible. I also had to calculate the proper popup width and image height to maintain a good visual balance—too large and the popup would cover the entire map, too small and the images wouldn't be impactful.

Conclusion

Creating this hometown map was a deeply personal and technical journey. It pushed me to think like a cartographer—considering not just what data to show, but how to tell a story with it. The project reinforced the importance of accuracy, especially when working with real-world geographic data, and taught me the value of sometimes taking manual approaches when automated solutions fall short.

On the technical side, this project expanded my skills beyond Python data processing into frontend web mapping with Mapbox GL JS. I learned that different tools have different strengths, and sometimes the "best" tool for a job isn't always the most obvious one. The combination of careful coordinate verification, thoughtful visual design, and clean technical implementation resulted in a map that I'm proud of—one that genuinely communicates why these places matter to me.

Looking back, I realize that this map is more than just a visualization; it's a digital artifact of my identity and connection to my city. Every location has a story, every marker represents a memory, and collectively they paint a picture of who I am and where I come from.
