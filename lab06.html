My Hometown Map: Saigon

Kayla Duong | DCDA 40833 Skills Portfolio | Spring 2026

Lab Synopsis

For this lab, I created an interactive hometown map that combines cartography, design, and personal storytelling. The goal was to build a map-based project that not only displays geographic information, but also reflects my understanding of place, identity, and visual communication. This lab demonstrates my ability to organize location-based data, think through design choices like color and symbol use, and reflect on both the technical and creative challenges of building an interactive map.

🗺️ Hometown Map - Saigon Locations

An interactive map showcasing 12 meaningful locations in Saigon, Vietnam. Each marker represents a place that has shaped who I am - from schools and favorite restaurants to cultural landmarks and recreational spots where I've spent countless hours with friends and family. Click on any marker to discover more about that location, including photos and personal stories about why each place matters to me.

💡 Tip: Use the navigation controls on the map to zoom in/out and explore different areas of Saigon. The legend in the bottom-left corner shows you what each colored icon represents!

About This Map

Saigon is more than just my hometown - it's a living, breathing city full of energy, contradictions, and endless possibilities. This map is my way of sharing a piece of my identity through the places that have shaped me. Each of the 12 locations represents a chapter in my story, organized into five categories: Schools & Educational Institutions, Landmarks, Restaurants & Cafes, Culture & History, and Recreation.

I created this map not just as a portfolio project, but as a love letter to my city. It's my way of saying thank you to Saigon for the education, the experiences, the friendships, and the countless moments - both big and small - that have shaped my life.

Reflection & Analysis

Design Inspiration

The design of this map was inspired by a Vietnamese spring picnic artwork - three women in áo dài sitting by a lake surrounded by blossoms and greenery. What I loved about it was how soft and calm the palette feels, even though there are still a lot of details. Everything looks "layered" but not chaotic, and I wanted to translate that feeling into my map.

I based my custom Mapbox basemap on this aesthetic by using muted greens as the primary land colors to mimic the gardens and foliage in the artwork, and a gentle aqua tone for water to match the peaceful lake in the background. For built areas and secondary features, I kept warmer cream and light neutral tones inspired by the flowers, picnic items, and overall glow of the scene, so the map feels airy instead of heavy. I also used a warmer accent color (inspired by the orange áo dài) in small ways to add contrast and guide the eye without overpowering the soft base palette.

For the markers themselves, I chose emoji icons because they're universally recognizable and add a playful, personal touch to the cartography. The five location categories - schools (blue 🏫), landmarks (orange 🏢), restaurants (green 🍽️), culture (purple 🏛️), and recreation (red 🎾) - emerged naturally from the types of places that are most meaningful to me. This color coding helps users quickly understand what they're looking at without needing to read the legend every time.

Overall, my goal was for the map to feel like a "spring in Saigon" mood - peaceful, nostalgic, and painterly - rather than a standard, high-contrast navigation map. I wanted visitors to feel the same sense of calm and layered beauty that I felt looking at that artwork, while still being able to explore and understand the meaningful places in my life.

Cartographic Challenges

One of my biggest cartographic challenges was determining the exact coordinates for each location. Vietnamese addresses are formatted differently than addresses in English-speaking countries, which made automated geocoding unreliable. When I initially used the Mapbox Geocoding API, several locations were placed in completely wrong areas, some even ended up outside of Saigon!

To solve this, I manually verified each location using Google Maps and collected the precise latitude/longitude coordinates. This manual verification process took time, but it ensured accuracy. Another challenge was deciding on the zoom level and center of the map as I wanted to show all 12 locations while keeping the view zoomed in enough to see Saigon clearly, so I settled on a zoom level of 12 and centered it on the heart of District 1.

I also grappled with the popup design. I wanted to include images for each location to make it visually rich, but I had to balance image file sizes with loading performance. Ultimately, I set a fixed height for popup images and used responsive sizing to keep popups from becoming overwhelming on smaller screens.

Technical Challenges

The biggest technical challenge was switching from Folium (Python-based mapping library) to Mapbox GL JS. My initial approach used Folium with a Mapbox tileset, but the custom Mapbox style wasn't displaying correctly through Folium's TileLayer implementation. After troubleshooting and researching, I realized that Mapbox GL JS was the proper solution for using modern Mapbox custom styles.

This required a complete rewrite of the map implementation from scratch using JavaScript instead of Python. I worked with Claude to implement this transition, and I was impressed by how well Claude understood my directions and requirements. Claude didn't just generate code - it asked clarifying questions about my vision (like how I wanted the popups to look, what color scheme I preferred, and how important responsive design was), and then implemented solutions that matched my exact specifications. When I needed to make adjustments (like increasing image sizes in popups, adding a legend box, or fixing marker placements), Claude efficiently incorporated my feedback without losing the overall structure.

Together, we hardcoded all 12 location objects with their coordinates, descriptions, and image URLs directly into the HTML/JavaScript. I also had to manually verify coordinates using Google Maps since automated geocoding was unreliable for Vietnamese addresses, Claude helped me set up the geocoding process and debug the coordinate issues when locations were appearing in wrong locations initially.

Another technical challenge was managing popups with embedded images. We implemented proper error handling (using the onerror attribute) in case any image URLs broke or became inaccessible. This iterative process of refinement really benefited from Claude's ability to understand and implement nuanced design feedback.

Conclusion

Creating this hometown map was a deeply personal and technical journey. It pushed me to think like a cartographer - considering not just what data to show, but how to tell a story with it. The project reinforced the importance of accuracy, especially when working with real-world geographic data, and taught me the value of sometimes taking manual approaches when automated solutions fall short.

Looking back, I realize that this map is more than just a visualization; it's a digital artifact of my identity and connection to my city. Every location has a story, every marker represents a memory, and collectively they paint a picture of who I am and where I come from.
